# Maximal Ideals and Fields

## Definition

Let $R$ be a ring. A proper ideal $M$ of $R$ is called a **maximal ideal** if there is no ideal $I$ such that $M \subsetneq I \subsetneq R$.

## Main Theorem

**Theorem.** Let $R$ be a commutative ring with unity and let $M$ be an ideal of $R$. Then $M$ is a maximal ideal if and only if $R/M$ is a field.

## Proof

We prove both directions.

### Forward Direction: $M$ maximal $\Rightarrow$ $R/M$ is a field

Assume $M$ is a maximal ideal of $R$.

Since $M$ is a proper ideal, we have $1 \notin M$. Therefore, $1 + M \neq 0 + M$ in $R/M$, so $R/M$ is nontrivial.

Now let $\overline{a} = a + M$ be a nonzero element of $R/M$. Then $a \notin M$.

Consider the ideal $I = M + (a) = \{m + ra : m \in M, r \in R\}$, which is the ideal generated by $M$ and $a$.

Since $a \notin M$, we have $M \subsetneq I$. But $M$ is maximal, so we must have $I = R$.

Therefore, $1 \in I$, which means:

$$
1 = m + ra
$$

for some $m \in M$ and $r \in R$.

Taking cosets modulo $M$:

$$
1 + M = (m + M) + r(a + M) = 0 + M + r \cdot \overline{a} = r \cdot \overline{a}
$$

Thus $\overline{r} \cdot \overline{a} = \overline{1}$, showing that $\overline{a}$ has a multiplicative inverse in $R/M$.

Since every nonzero element of $R/M$ has a multiplicative inverse, $R/M$ is a field.

### Backward Direction: $R/M$ is a field $\Rightarrow$ $M$ is maximal

Assume $R/M$ is a field.

Let $I$ be an ideal of $R$ such that $M \subseteq I \subseteq R$. We need to show that either $I = M$ or $I = R$.

By the correspondence theorem, $I$ corresponds to an ideal $\overline{I} = I/M$ of $R/M$.

Since $R/M$ is a field, its only ideals are $\{0 + M\}$ and $R/M$ itself.

Therefore, either $\overline{I} = \{0 + M\}$ or $\overline{I} = R/M$.

If $\overline{I} = \{0 + M\}$, then $I = M$.

If $\overline{I} = R/M$, then for any $r \in R$, we have $r + M \in \overline{I}$, so $r \in I$. Thus $I = R$.

Therefore, $M$ is maximal.

## Conclusion

We have shown that for a commutative ring $R$ with unity and an ideal $M$ of $R$:

$$
M \text{ is maximal} \iff R/M \text{ is a field}
$$

This is a fundamental result in ring theory with important applications to polynomial rings and algebraic geometry.
